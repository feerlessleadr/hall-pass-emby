How to use Hall Pass

  1. Scan. Press "Scan now" below. This is safe while dry run is on: a scan only records what Hall Pass finds and never changes your files.
  2. Review. Open the Review tab and work through "Needs review". Mark tracks explicit or clean, or mark a whole album or artist at once. Your choices are saved, and a scan never overwrites them. You can stop and come back at any time, and you don't have to finish before step 5: tracks still in review simply get the "explicit-review" tag.
  3. Preview. Press "Preview changes" below to see exactly which tags would be written.
  4. Turn dry run off. Go to Settings > Tags & writing, untick "Dry run" and save. You don't need to scan again.
  5. Apply. Press "Apply decisions" below. Every change is recorded, so you can undo it from the History tab. If you change some decisions later, apply again: only the differences are written.
Dry run is on. Hall Pass records scans and your decisions, but it writes no tags to your library. To write tags, preview the changes, untick "Dry run" under Settings > Tags & writing, and then press Apply.

Your changes to the word list

Hall Pass comes with a built-in word list in three tiers: severe words (each counts 3), moderate words (each counts 2) and mild words (recorded, but they count for nothing). Add words here, or move a built-in word to another tier, with one word per line. A word you list takes the tier of the box it is in and replaces its tier in the built-in list. Inflections and masked spellings (like sh*t) are caught automatically.
Hall Pass removes these words from the scan entirely.

Your files

Uses Emby's own ffprobe on this server. It finds an explicit or clean flag when a file has one, plus the ISRC and album barcode that make online lookups precise. Nothing leaves your server.

Online lookups

Off by default. When on, Hall Pass asks Deezer (api.deezer.com) about a track by its ISRC, or by artist and title, and Apple iTunes (itunes.apple.com) about its album by barcode. It sends only the artist, title, ISRC or album barcode, and never file names, paths, lyrics or anything about you or your server. Answers are kept on this server so nothing is asked twice, and a failed lookup never makes a track look clean. Turn this off and nothing leaves your server.
iTunes allows about 20 questions a minute, so a first scan with iTunes is slow on a big library (one question per album). Deezer allows far more.

Lyrics for tracks that have none

Off by default, and only used while online lookups are on. When a track has no lyrics of its own, Hall Pass asks lrclib.net for them, sending only the artist, title, album and length. It scans the text in memory and never keeps it, only the verdict. Because LRCLIB matches by name, its evidence counts for less than the file's own lyrics: on its own it can make a track likely clean or send it to review, but never certain.
After you change anything here, press "Rescan everything" on the Dashboard so Hall Pass looks at your existing tracks again.

Albums and artists

Hall Pass can also tag albums and artists, so a playlist can leave out a whole album or artist that contains explicit music. These changes are recorded in the History tab, so you can undo them like track tags.
Off by default. When you press Apply, an album gets the explicit tag if at least one of its tracks is explicit, and loses it again if none are. Albums only ever get the explicit tag.
Off by default. This works the same way for artists: every artist credited on an explicit track gets the explicit tag.

Scheduled scan

Hall Pass adds a scheduled task, Dashboard > Scheduled Tasks > Hall Pass > Scan music for explicit content. It runs every night at 3:30 and looks only at new or changed tracks. You can change or remove its schedule there, like any other task.

New tracks

When Emby adds music, Hall Pass waits until things have been quiet for half a minute and then looks at the new tracks, so a big import is handled in small batches. It never runs at the same time as another Hall Pass job.
